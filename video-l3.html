VIDEO PRODUCTION · LESSON 3

Using a storyboard

Last lesson you filmed with a shot list in your hand. Today you plan a whole video on paper, so nobody has to guess what the camera does next.

Why not just start filming?

01Nobody filmed a close-up of the hero’s face.
02Two people film the same shot; nobody films the ending.
03The battery dies and nobody knows which shots are left.
A storyboard is a comic strip of your video, drawn before anyone films.

One panel, six jobs

Movement. An arrow for a pan or tilt. No arrow, no movement.

5

The sound. Speech, music or a noise.

6

The time. How many seconds it lasts.

Draw it fast, and draw it clearly

Stick people are fineThe camera operator needs to read it, not frame it
Wide rectanglesDraw each panel the same shape as the screen
Show how bigTiny person in a wide shot; a face that fills the frame in a close-up
Arrows for movementAcross the panel for a pan, up or down for a tilt
Number every panelSo the order can’t get mixed up
Words go underneathLabels stay outside the frame, so the picture stays clear

Four panels with a problem

1
(no label)The hero walks into the room
Wide, mid or close? The camera operator has to guess.
2
Close-up · 3 sThen another close-up, then another, all of the same face
Three of the same shot in a row. Does the second one add anything?
3
Mid shot · 5 sThe hero runs across the table, climbs the lamp and jumps off
Three actions in one panel. Split it into three.
4
Mid shotThe camera follows the hero along the shelf
The words say the camera moves, but there is no arrow and no time.

Finished all six panels?

01Pick the panel you like least and redraw it from a different angle.
02Write the first line anyone speaks, and add it to that panel’s sound.
03Add up your seconds. If you’re outside 25 to 35, what could you cut or stretch?
