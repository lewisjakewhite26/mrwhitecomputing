# Year 3/4 Autumn 2: Animation

Planned 28 September 2026. Source: the Teach Computing Year 3 Stop-frame
animation pack (six lessons, slides last updated August 2024). End points:
`end-points.md`, Year 3/4 Autumn 2. The pack's Roman characters and
backgrounds fit the Cycle B Autumn topic, Invasion, so they stay.

## The sequence

The pack's L5 (evaluate) and L6 (iMovie and a tourism advert) are replaced.
Improving happens while filming, and the unit ends with pupils reviewing their
own film on camera.

| Lesson | What happens | End points |
|---|---|---|
| L1 The history of animation | Stop motion history, from a YouTube video Lewis is choosing, and making a thaumatrope. Offline | Y3 animation is a sequence of drawings or photographs |
| L2 Flipbooks and zoetropes | Flipbooks and a paper zoetrope with a 12-frame strip. Offline | Y3 animation is a sequence; Y3 relate movement with a sequence of images |
| L3 Planning an animation | Roman storyboard, "which plan is achievable?", counting frames | Y3 and Y4 plan an animation |
| L4 Filming an animation | iMotion in pairs, with a compulsory review stop | Y3 relate movement with images; Y4 work consistently and carefully; Y4 review and improve |
| L5 Editing an animation | Reshoot weak scenes, add a title and music | Y4 consistently and carefully; Y4 review and improve; Y4 impact of other media |
| L6 Reviewing an animation | Voiceover commentary video in iMovie | Y4 impact of other media |

## Why these changes

Frame rate is the biggest gap in the pack: iMotion has a speed setting and the
pack only says "choose an appropriate speed", yet speed is how a sequence of
pictures becomes movement. The zoetrope teaches it by hand. Spin slowly and it
flickers; spin faster and it goes smooth. L3 then puts a number on it: a
5-second film at 10 frames a second needs 50 photos, which gives "is this
achievable?" something to check against.

About 15 minutes into L4 everyone stops, plays back what they have, checks it
against the consistency checklist and deletes or reshoots at least one frame.
Without a fixed stop, "improve as you go" doesn't happen.

L6's commentary uses voiceover over the animation, not faces. It looks like
a YouTube breakdown and avoids filming children on shared iPads. Pupils need
a structure or every video says "it went well":

1. What we planned
2. What went wrong while filming
3. What we changed, and how
4. What the music or title adds, and how we know
5. What we'd do differently

Point 4 is the Year 4 end point; insist on it. Finish with a premiere of the
best few on the board.

## To check before half term

Assign one numbered iPad per pair for the whole unit; L4 to L6 depend on the
same camera roll. Leave sets up between L4 and L5, or a moved background makes
the film jump. Check there are stands or tripods for around 15 pairs, that
iMotion and iMovie are installed, and that the chosen YouTube video plays on
the school network. The pack's videos are short but embedded in the slides,
which is why the zips run to around 1.4 GB; compress them before they go in
the app.
