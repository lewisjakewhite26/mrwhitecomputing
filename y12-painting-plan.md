# Year 1/2 Autumn 2: Digital painting

Planned 28 September 2026. Source: the Teach Computing Year 1 Digital painting
pack (six lessons, last updated August 2021). End points: `end-points.md`,
Year 1/2 Autumn 2.

## Decisions

Pupils use iPads this half term. The unit runs on our own paint app,
`paint.html`, built alongside `quick-click.html` and `whiteflix.html`, not on
Microsoft Paint or PaintZ. Confidence checks reuse the ones built for Autumn 1
and happen together on the board.

The pack stays as the spine. It's well sequenced, but it teaches art with a
computer rather than computing, and nearly every check is a child rating
themselves. The changes below fix both.

## The paint app

One rule decides everything else: the tools behave like real paint software.
Fill still leaks through a gap in a shape, the rubber still paints white, and
undo steps back through a list of actions. If our version is friendlier than
the real thing, the computing ideas disappear and nothing transfers to Paint
later.

The teacher opens the app in a mode for each lesson, and each mode shows only
the tools that lesson needs:

| Lesson | Mode |
|---|---|
| L1 Freehand tools | Pencil, brush, spray, fill, rubber, undo |
| L2 Mondrian | Rectangle and straight line; palette locked to red, yellow, blue, black and white |
| L3 Matisse | Cut-out stampers: leaf, wave and spiral shapes to place, turn and resize. Matisse cut and arranged painted paper, so this is his method, not a shortcut |
| L4 Kandinsky | Everything unlocked, no circle stamper, because "brush or shape tool?" is the lesson's question |
| L5 Seurat | Dotting brush in three sizes, plus the spray can |

It records which tools each child used, so Year 2s can explain their choices
from evidence ("fill 6 times, rectangle 4 times"). Replay plays a picture back
stroke by stroke, which answers L6's question about what a computer can do
that paint can't.

Pictures save on the iPad under the child's first name, picked from a class
list. That removes the L5 to L6 file dependency that already broke once in
Autumn 1. A "send to the board" button could reuse the live answer wall
backend, but it waits for the data protection sign-off in `TODO.md`.

Risks to test on a real iPad early: school filtering (make it work offline
once loaded), shared iPads, and Safari zooming the page on a stray pinch or a
palm.

## Lesson changes

L1 keeps the free exploration and the fill leaking out of an unclosed circle,
but turns the leak into "predict what happens" before the teacher fills. Add a
quick check such as "Which tool made this?".

L2 drops the artist's dates, which mean nothing at six. Replace the tick sheet
with a check that has right answers.

L3 is called "Making careful choices" and then models the Matisse copy step by
step. Give a real choice instead ("make a snail from any shapes you like") and
have Year 2s explain one of their choices.

L4 keeps the multiple-choice questions on slides 4 to 12, which are the best
assessment in the pack; spread them across the unit as Reboot starters. Cut
the paper feelings task. It drifts into PSHE and the lesson can't hold it.

L5 allows the spray can as well as the brush. Thirty minutes of single dots
with a finger is hard work for Year 1.

L6 keeps the cotton-bud painting and the class vote (which leads into Grouping
Data in Spring 2), but changes the question from "which do you prefer?" to
"what can the computer do that paint can't, and the other way round?".

End the unit with a class gallery. Each child picks their best picture, and
Year 2s write a label saying which tools they used and why. That carries the
Year 2 end points and gives the work an audience.

## Also

Matisse died in 1954, so his UK copyright ran out at the end of 2024. The
pack's DACS notice is out of date. Use Wikimedia Commons scans for all four
artists rather than the pack's Google Images links.
