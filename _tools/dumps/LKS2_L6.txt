Lesson 6: What does our school network look like?
Introduction
In this lesson, learners will further develop their understanding of computer networks. They will see examples of network infrastructure in a real-world setting and relate them to the activities in Lesson 5.
Learning objectives
To recognise the physical components of a network
I can identify how devices in a network are connected together
I can identify networked devices around me
I can identify the benefits of computer networks
Key vocabulary
Network cables, network sockets
Preparation
Subject knowledge:
You will need to be familiar with how the main devices (network switch, server, wireless access point, router, and printer or copier) on your school’s network are connected with one another, and where the devices are located. You may wish to discuss this with the IT technician prior to the lesson or even better, invite your school IT technician in to support the lesson, if possible.
You will need to know that network cables and network sockets are needed to connect devices in a network.
You will need:
Slides
Activities:
A2 Activity sheet – Scavenger hunt (optional)
A3 Activity sheet – Mapping a network
A camera, or a tablet that can take photographs (optional)
Assessment opportunities
Activity 1: You can assess whether learners can explain the benefits of computer networks.
Activity 2: You can assess whether learners can identify real-world network devices.
Activity 3: You can assess whether learners can explain how different components are connected with one another in a computer network.
Outline plan
Please note that the slide deck labels the activities in the top right-hand corner to help you navigate the lesson. 
*Timings are rough guides
Introduction
(Slides 1–6)
5 mins
Display slide 2 and introduce the learning objective and success criteria.
Click through the animations on slides 3 to 5 to remind learners of three network components that were introduced in the previous lessons. 
Ask learners to discuss what the network components that were introduced in the previous lessons do.
Display slide 6. Explain to learners that a number of other components are part of a network, including:
Network (Ethernet) cables
Network (Ethernet) sockets
Click through the animations on slide 6 to show learners the definitions of ‘network cable’ and ‘network socket’.
Activity 1
(Slide 7)
5 mins
Discuss the benefits of computer networks
Ask learners the following questions. Once learners have discussed these questions, invite them to give feedback to the class:
What kind of tasks does the school network let learners and staff members do?Possible responses: On the school network, files can be accessed and shared, staff members can access the register and administration system, and messages can be sent easily between people. Note: Learners may also talk about being able to access the internet. This is possible on most networks, however, a computer does not have to be part of a local network to connect to the internet, as is the case with many home computers.
Why is it useful for more than one person to be able to access information in school?Possible responses: It is useful because it means that information such as attendance information or shared projects can be accessed by more than one person, meaning that such information only needs to be entered once. Communication between staff members and learners can be more effective.
Activity 2
(Slides 8-9)
15 mins
Look at the school network
Display slide 8, which introduces pupils to the role of the schools IT technician (you may wish to personalise the slide by replacing the image with a photograph of your technician). If you have invited your technician to support this part of the lesson, please ask them to explain in their own words, what their role is and if appropriate, invite questions from pupils, who are interested to know more about the job. 
Move on to slide 9 (ideally your technician will be on hand to support this next activity).
Explain to learners that they are going to go on a brief tour of the school to find the key components of the computer network discussed in the unit so far:
Devices
Printers or copiers
Wireless access points (WAPs)
Network (Ethernet) cables
Network (Ethernet) socket
Switch
Server
You could ask learners to take photographs of the various network components. They may also find other network devices, such as routers or firewalls.
During the activity, remind learners how information moves around a network. You could give the example of opening a file from the server on the class computer: the computer sends a request for the file through the wired or wireless network to the switch, which sends the request to the server, then the server replies, sending the file back through the switch to the class computer.
Note: During the tour, learners might mention the internet at home, or the school internet connection. You will address this topic during the Plenary.
Scaffolding opportunity: You could adapt this activity into an ‘I spy’ game, scavenger hunt, or tally chart, and you could give learners images of the devices that they need to find. You could use the A2 Activity sheet for this, adapting it for your chosen activity.
Explorer task: As learners are finding the different network components, ask them to consider the purpose of each device:
What is the device’s job in the school network?
Is it vital to the network or optional?
Activity 3
(Slide 10)
15 mins
Map your school network
Display slide 10. Remind learners how the parts of a network that they have seen during their network tour combine to form a network. Click through the animations on the slide to show how the following are connected:
Switch, server, and printer
Desktop computers
Wireless devices (laptop computers and tablet computers)
Ask learners to amend the network map on the A3 Activity sheet with where those devices were found in school.
Plenary
(Slides 11–12)
5 mins
Discussion
Display slide 11. Ask learners to recap what a computer network is, and to discuss how networks can help us. For example, learners might say that they can share files, and all staff members can access the register.
What about the internet?
Display slide 12. During Activity 2, learners may have mentioned the internet at home, or the school internet connection. Explain to learners that the school network is connected to a router. The router provides a connection to the internet through an external cable.
Conclusion
(Slides 13–14)
5 mins
Review the ‘Assessment’ and ‘Summary’ slides.
