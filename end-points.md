# Computing end points, Cycle B

Transcribed from `COMPUTING Cycle B END POINTS Updated 2024`, the school
curriculum document. Collected 23 September 2026.

This is the authority for what each unit has to teach. When a slide, activity
or tool in `index.html` claims to serve an end point, check it here first. The
wording below is the document's own, not a paraphrase.

Each year group has six units, two per term. Autumn 1 is the first unit listed
and is the one the current deck covers for all three year groups.

## Year 1/2, Cycle B

Autumn: Movers & Shakers. Spring: Coastline. Summer: Magnificent Monarchs.

### Autumn 1: Technology around us

Digital literacy, connecting systems and networks. Suggested software:
Paintz.app.

Enquiry question: what technology do we find in school and how do we use it
responsibly?

Year 1 and Year 2 have the same six end points for this unit. There is no
progression written between the year groups.

- To identify technology
- To identify a computer and its main parts
- To use a mouse in different ways
- To use a keyboard to type on a computer
- To use the keyboard to edit text
- To create rules for using technology responsibly

### Autumn 2: Digital painting

Information technology, creating media. Microsoft Paint or similar.

Year 1:
- To describe what different freehand tools do
- To use the shape tool and the line tools
- To use a computer on my own to paint a picture

Year 2:
- To make careful choices when painting a digital picture
- To explain why I chose the tools I used
- To compare painting a picture on a computer and on paper

### Spring 1: Moving a robot

Computer science, programming block A. Bee-bot or Blue-bot.

Year 1:
- To explain what a given command will do
- To act out a given word
- To combine forwards and backwards commands to make a sequence
- To plan a simple program

Year 2:
- To combine four direction commands in a simple program
- To plan a simple program
- To find more than one solution to a problem

### Spring 2: Grouping data

Information technology, data and information. Google Slides or PowerPoint.

Year 1:
- To label objects
- To identify that objects can be counted
- To count objects with the same properties
- To answer questions about groups of objects

Year 2:
- To compare groups of objects
- To answer questions about groups of objects
- Input information using a date handling program

### Summer 1: Digital writing

Information technology, creating media. Google Docs or Microsoft Word.

Year 1:
- To use a computer to write
- To add and remove text on a computer
- To identify that the look of text can be changed on a computer

Year 2:
- To make careful choices when changing text
- To explain why I used the tools that I chose
- To compare typing on a computer to writing on paper

### Summer 2: Programming animations

Computer science, programming block B. Scratch Jr.

Year 1:
- To describe a simple program on Scratch Jnr
- To identify the effect of changing a value in Scratch Jnr

Year 2:
- To explain that each sprite has its own instructions
- To design the parts of a project
- To use my algorithm to create a program

### Cumulative skills for the year

Year 1:
- To begin to plan and test their instructions
- To use digital technology to organise and edit content (e.g., data in a
  graph, editing images)

Year 2:
- To appreciate that some algorithms are more efficient than others
- To consider when digital technology leads to improvements or has the
  potential to make things worse
- To use digital technology to organise and edit content (e.g., data in a
  graph, editing images)

## Year 3/4, Cycle B

Autumn: Invasion. Spring: Misty Mountain, Winding River. Summer: Ancient
Civilisations.

### Autumn 1: Connecting computers

Digital literacy, connecting systems and networks. Painting program.

Enquiry question: what devices have inputs, processes, and outputs?

Year 3:
- To explain how digital devices function
- To identify input and output devices
- To recognise how digital devices can change the way we work

Year 4:
- To explain how a computer network can be used to share information
- To explore how digital devices can be connected
- To recognise the physical components of a network

### Autumn 2: Stop frame animation

Information technology, creating media. iMotion.

Year 3:
- To explain that animation is a sequence of drawings or photographs
- To relate animated movement with a sequence of images
- To plan an animation

Year 4:
- To plan an animation
- To identify the need to work consistently and carefully
- To review and improve an animation
- To evaluate the impact of adding other media to an animation

### Spring 1: Sequencing sounds

Computer science, programming block A. Scratch.

Year 3:
- To identify that commands have an outcome
- To explain that a program has a start
- To recognise that a sequence of commands can have an order

Year 4:
- To recognise that a sequence of commands can have an order
- To change the appearance of my project
- To create a project from a task description

### Spring 2: Branching databases

Information technology, data and information. j2data Branch and Pictogram.

Year 3:
- To create questions with yes/no answers
- To identify the attributes needed to collect data about an object
- To create a branching database

Year 4:
- To create a branching database
- To plan the structure of a branching database
- To independently create an identification tool

### Summer 1: Desktop publishing

Information technology, creating media. Canva.com.

Year 3:
- To recognise how text and images convey information
- To recognise that text and layout can be edited
- To add content to a desktop publishing publication

Year 4:
- To choose appropriate page settings
- To consider how different layouts can suit different purposes
- To consider the benefits of desktop publishing

### Summer 2: Events and actions in programs

Computer science, programming block B. Scratch.

Year 3:
- To create a program to move a sprite in four directions
- To adapt a program to a new context
- To develop my program by adding features

Year 4:
- To identify and fix bugs in a program
- To design and create a maze-based challenge

### Cumulative skills for the year

Year 3:
- To recognise the impact of keyword choice on search engine results (e.g.,
  results ranked according to relevance)
- To evaluate content (created researched) against a given goal

Year 4:
- To design and create content on a computer in response to a given goal,
  paying attention to the needs of a known audience
- To give reasons for errors in programs and explain how they have corrected
  these
- To explain an algorithm using sequence, repetition and selection in their own
  words

## Year 5/6, Cycle B

Autumn: Maafa. Spring: Frozen Kingdoms. Summer: Britain at War.

### Autumn 1: Systems and searching

Digital literacy, connecting systems and networks. Google Slides.

Enquiry question: what IT systems are around the world and how do they help us
search the internet?

Year 5:
- To explain that computers can be connected together to form systems
- To recognise the role of computer systems in our lives
- To experiment with search engines
- To describe how search engines select results

Year 6:
- To describe how search engines select results
- To explain how search results are ranked
- To recognise why the order of results is important, and to whom

### Autumn 2: Video production

Information technology, creating media. Microsoft Photos.

Year 5:
- To explain what makes a video effective
- To identify digital devices that can record video
- To capture video using a range of techniques
- To create a storyboard

Year 6:
- To capture video using a range of techniques
- To identify that video can be improved through reshooting and editing
- To consider the impact of the choices made when making and sharing a video

### Spring 1: Variables in games

Computer science, programming block A. Scratch.

Year 5:
- To define a 'variable' as something that is changeable
- To explain why a variable is used in a program
- To improve a game by using variables

Year 6:
- To design a project that builds on a given example
- To use my design to create a project
- To evaluate my project

### Spring 2: Introduction to spreadsheets

Information technology, data and information. Google Sheets or Excel.

Year 5:
- To create a data set in a spreadsheet
- To build a data set in a spreadsheet
- To explain that formulas can be used to produce calculated data

Year 6:
- To apply formulas to data
- To create a spreadsheet to plan an event
- To choose suitable ways to present data

### Summer 1: 3D modelling

Information technology, creating media. Tinkercad.

Year 5:
- To recognise that you can work in three dimensions on a computer
- To identify that digital 3D objects can be modified
- To create a 3D model for a given purpose

Year 6:
- To create a 3D model for a given purpose
- To plan my own 3D model
- To create my own digital 3D model

### Summer 2: Selection in quizzes

Computer science, programming block. Scratch.

Year 5 and Year 6 have the same six end points for this unit.

- To explain how selection is used in computer programs
- To relate that a conditional statement connects a condition to an outcome
- To explain how selection directs the flow of a program
- To design a program which uses selection
- To create a program which uses selection
- To evaluate my program

### Cumulative skills for the year

Year 5:
- To make a multimedia presentation that contains: sound; animation; video and
  buttons to navigate
- To save an image document as a gif or jpeg file format using the command
  (e.g. "save as")
- To evaluate content according to its effectiveness and impact on a target
  audience
- To write programs that have sequences, repetitions and variables
- To consider the audience when editing a simple film and justify their choices

Year 6:
- To incorporate graphics where appropriate, using the most effective text
  wrapping formats
- To compare the information provided on two tabbed websites looking for bias
  and perspective
- To check and refine a series of instructions

## The online safety strand

Online safety runs alongside the units, one or two strands per unit slot, in
the same order for every year group:

| Unit slot | Strands |
|---|---|
| Autumn 1 | Self-image and identity; online relationships |
| Autumn 2 | Online reputation |
| Spring 1 | Online bullying |
| Spring 2 | Managing online information |
| Summer 1 | Health, well-being and lifestyle |
| Summer 2 | Privacy and security; copyright and ownership |

This matters for Year 1/2 Lesson 6. Passwords and pop-up scams sit under
privacy and security, which is a Summer 2 strand. Teaching them when they come
up is fine, but the Autumn work in that lesson is the responsibility and care
end point, not e-safety.

## Notes for the deck

Points worth carrying into planning, rather than things the document says:

- **Year 3 carries a searching end point with no searching unit.** "Recognise
  the impact of keyword choice on search engine results" is a Year 3 cumulative
  skill, but Systems and searching is a Year 5/6 unit. Anything built to show
  Year 6 how a query becomes keywords covers this too.
- **The Year 2 cumulative skill about improvements and harm** is the clearest
  justification for asking Year 1/2 to judge whether a use of technology is a
  good idea, rather than only naming rules.
- **Two units have no progression written between the paired year groups:**
  Year 1/2 Technology around us, and Year 5/6 Selection in quizzes. Both lists
  are identical. That is the document's gap, not a planning error further down.
