# Year 5/6 Autumn 2: Vector drawing

Planned 28 September 2026. Source: the Teach Computing Year 5 Vector drawing
pack (six lessons, slides last updated October 2024). It's a Year 5 unit, so
Year 6 need stretch throughout.

The school's end points document has nothing for Vector Drawing (it still
lists Video production for Autumn 2; see the note in `end-points.md`). The
unit uses the pack's six lesson objectives as its end points.

## Software

Keynote on iPad. It's free, works offline and needs no pupil accounts, which
avoids the Google Drawings access problems the pack itself admits to. Shapes
and lines are genuine vector objects: pinch into a circle and it stays sharp,
pinch into a pasted photo and it breaks into pixels.

Pupils use shapes and lines only. Keynote's Draw tools (pen, pencil, crayon,
fill) make freehand drawings that can't become shapes, so they're off limits
for the unit. So are the ready-made shapes in the library beyond the basic
ones, and Generate Shape if the iPads have a Creator Studio subscription.
Turn alignment guides on in each iPad's Settings before L3. Grouping and Match
Size need one finger holding while another taps; model it and give practice
time, because this is where lessons will stall.

Keynote on iPad has no size guides (use Match Size instead) and doesn't
document point editing. Neither matters for this unit.

## The project

The pack is a software manual: each lesson adds a tool, and pupils spend most
of the unit copying drawings someone else made. One project runs through all
six lessons instead: a steam engine from the Stockton and Darlington Railway,
which fits the Revolution topic and opened a few miles from Hartburn in 1825.
It's circles and rectangles, the parts fit together obviously, and weaker
pupils can still finish something that looks right. The pack's copying tasks
shrink to 5-minute warm-ups.

| Lesson | Tool | On the engine |
|---|---|---|
| L1 Drawing with shapes | Shapes, colour, vector vs pixel | Boiler, chimney, cab and two wheels |
| L2 Copying and rotating | Copy, resize, rotate | Copy the wheels; rotate the connecting rods |
| L3 Adding detail | Zoom, alignment guides, borders, Match Size | Line the wheels up on the track |
| L4 Layers | Arrange forward and backward | Wheels behind the body, rods in front, smoke over the chimney |
| L5 Grouping objects | Group, copy, flip | Group a carriage, copy it into a train |
| L6 Making a poster | Everything | An opening-day poster in the flat-colour railway poster style |

A William Morris-inspired tile is the stretch for anyone who finishes early:
a simple motif of about 15 shapes, copied, flipped and repeated. Morris is in
the Year 5/6 Autumn art curriculum. The real Morris designs are far too
detailed for these tools, so it stays "inspired by", and the tiles sit side by
side in a grid rather than as a true repeat.

## Lesson changes

L1 defines vector drawings as "made on a computer, made of lines and shapes",
which is true of paint pictures too. The real difference: a vector picture is
a list of objects, each with a shape, position, size and colour, and a paint
picture is a grid of coloured pixels. That's why vectors enlarge without
blurring. The pack leaves this until L6; it becomes the L1 hook instead, with
pinch-zoom on a shape and a photo.

L2's plenary on Ctrl+C, Ctrl+V and Ctrl+Z doesn't work on iPads. Ask about
the touch equivalents instead.

L6 loses the separate reflection sheet and becomes finishing, a short critique
and a check on the vector idea from L1.

Year 6 stretch: combining shapes (Unite, Intersect, Subtract, Exclude in
Format, Arrange), such as subtracting a rectangle to cut the cab window. On
the board, show the code behind a shape, something like
`<circle cx="50" cy="50" r="20" fill="gold">`, and change a number to change
the shape. Keynote can't show this, so it lives in the app.

## Kept from the pack

The good checks: "which shape will be filled?" (L1, including the one where
nothing is selected), which owl shape goes first (L2), which two penguins are
identical (L3), the six layer-order questions and the emoji fix (L4), and
"what went wrong?" (L5). Use several as Reboot starters.
